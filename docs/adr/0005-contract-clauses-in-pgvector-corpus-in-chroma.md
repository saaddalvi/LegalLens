# Contract Clauses in pgvector on Neon, the Corpus stays in Chroma

Users' Contracts are split into Clauses, embedded and retrieved for follow-up Queries in a Contract conversation. Those vectors live in Postgres (pgvector) on Neon, in the same database as the user, Contract and Contract conversation rows. The Corpus stays in Chroma. LegalLens therefore has two vector stores.

Contracts are per-user, private and long-lived until the user deletes them. The Corpus is shared, read-only and rebuilt from pinned sources (`build_corpus`), so losing it costs only a rebuild. Keeping Clauses next to their Contract rows gives three things Chroma on the Hugging Face Space cannot: storage that survives a Space restart (the Space's disk is not durable), isolation enforced by filtering every query on the Contract's id, and one transaction that deletes a Contract, or a whole account on Clerk's `user.deleted` webhook, along with all of its Clauses and vectors. Moving the Corpus into pgvector as well was rejected for now. It would put a network round trip and a Neon dependency on every `/ask` Query, which works today with no database, and it would mean re-measuring retrieval (ADR 0002, ADR 0003) for no user-visible gain.

The cost: two retrieval code paths, both using the same Qwen3 embedding model (ADR 0003). Changing that model means rebuilding the Corpus and re-embedding every stored Contract, and the two must never be mixed. Whole-Contract-in-context (about 20k tokens for a 30-page Contract) was considered for follow-ups and rejected in favour of Clause retrieval. The Contract brief alone is written from the whole Contract text, because it must cover every Clause.
